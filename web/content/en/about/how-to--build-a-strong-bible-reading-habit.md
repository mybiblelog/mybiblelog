# How to Build a Strong Bible Reading Habit

**The most reliable way to build a Bible reading habit is to read at the same time and place every day, start with an amount small enough to never skip, and keep a visible record of your progress.** Missing a day doesn't break a habit — giving up after missing a day does.

Many people want to read the Bible regularly but struggle to stay consistent. In a 2019 Lifeway Research survey, only about a third of Protestant churchgoers (32%) said they read the Bible every day, and a 2026 follow-up again found fewer than 1 in 3. The good news: consistency is a skill you can build, and research on habits and goals shows how.

## Why consistency matters more than volume

Research from the Center for Bible Engagement, based on surveys of about 40,000 people, found that people who engaged with the Bible **four or more days a week** reported markedly different outcomes than those who read less often — for example, 30% lower rates of feeling lonely and 32% lower rates of anger issues. The difference between reading one or two days a week was small; the change appeared at around four days.

This research shows an association, not a guarantee, but its lesson is practical: a short reading most days is worth more than a long reading once in a while.

## How long does it take to form a Bible reading habit?

You may have heard that it takes 21 days to form a habit. A well-known study by Phillippa Lally and colleagues at University College London tracked people forming new daily habits and found it took a **median of about 66 days** for a behavior to become automatic — with a wide range, from 18 to 254 days depending on the person and the habit.

Two findings from that study are especially encouraging:

* Habits form through **repetition in a consistent context** — the same time or the same trigger each day.
* **Missing a single day did not materially affect** habit formation. One missed day is not a failure.

So give yourself two to three months of steady practice, and don't panic if you slip.

## 7 ways to build a Bible reading habit

### 1. Tie your reading to an existing routine

Choose a specific cue you already do every day: "After I pour my morning coffee, I will open my Bible." Psychologist Peter Gollwitzer calls these **implementation intentions** — "when situation X arises, I will do Y" — and his research found that making this kind of specific plan substantially increases follow-through.

### 2. Start smaller than you think you need to

A goal you can meet on your worst day builds momentum. Start with one chapter, or 5 to 10 minutes, and increase later. The Navigators' popular 5x5x5 plan, for example, asks for just five minutes a day, five days a week.

### 3. Decide what you'll read before you sit down

Decision fatigue kills habits. Pick a book to work through, follow a [reading plan](/about/guide--bible-reading-plans-compared), or let My Bible Log's [Today page](/about/page-features--today) suggest what to read next based on what you haven't read yet.

### 4. Track your progress

A large meta-analysis of 138 studies found that **monitoring your progress makes you more likely to reach a goal** — and the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded. Checking off chapters, logging what you read, or filling in a chart all count.

### 5. Make it easy to recover from missed days

Plans that assign a specific passage to each calendar date can make one missed day feel like falling behind. Instead, aim for a **weekly** target (for example, 20 chapters a week) or use a tracker that records what you've read rather than what you "should" have read. My Bible Log counts whatever you read — from a plan, a sermon, or a small group — toward your progress, with no guilt for missed days.

### 6. Read with other people

Share your goal with a friend, spouse, or small group, and check in weekly. The same meta-analysis found that progress monitoring had a larger effect when progress was reported to others.

### 7. Aim for understanding, not just completion

A habit lasts longer when it's meaningful. Keep a short note of a question, a verse to remember, or something to pray about. Our guide to [organizing your personal Bible study](/about/how-to--organize-your-personal-bible-study) has seven ideas for using notes and tags.

## What if I've tried before and stopped?

Most people who build a lasting habit have restarted more than once. Start again today with a smaller goal, a specific cue, and a way to see your progress. If you want a concrete target, try the [New Testament in 90 days](/about/how-to--read-the-new-testament-in-90-days) or [the whole Bible in a year](/about/how-to--read-the-bible-in-a-year).
